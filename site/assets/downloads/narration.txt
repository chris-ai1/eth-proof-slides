AN ALGEBRAIC ESCAPE — WEBSITE NARRATION




01. The whole idea, before the machinery.

Purpose: independent small groups can be handled separately, but arbitrary SAT formulas contain interactions between groups.

The opportunity

We are here because independent small groups can be handled separately, but arbitrary SAT formulas contain interactions between groups. If every clause stayed within one group, we could solve the groups separately and multiply their counts. Crossing clauses prevent that separation. The useful idea is structural independence, not a numerical comparison of assignment counts.

$$
\text{independent groups}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{separate sums, then multiply}
$$

The transfer

Instead of separating the original formula directly, build a polynomial whose value at zero is its count. Other selected points will have more useful structure. Here F is the original formula. T will be the polynomial parameter, and H of T its weighted counting sum.

$$
H(0)=\#F
$$

The two jobs

We need a low-degree construction that creates structured points, and an affordable way to obtain enough data at those points. Interpolation then recovers the answer. These are different jobs. The first is an algebraic construction; the second is a derivative-recovery algorithm. Next we will isolate the obstruction: exactly how a crossing clause couples two groups.

$$
\text{construct structure}\quad+\quad\text{recover enough data}
$$


02. Why drawing boxes does not help.

Purpose: we need to know what must change before a group-by-group calculation is valid.

Separate choices

We are here because we need to know what must change before a group-by-group calculation is valid. For genuinely independent groups, the sum of a product factors into a product of sums. The variables x sub A and x sub B are the assignments in the two groups.

$$
\sum_{x_A,x_B} f(x_A)g(x_B)=\left(\sum_{x_A}f(x_A)\right)\left(\sum_{x_B}g(x_B)\right)
$$

A crossing factor

A factor involving both groups prevents this identity from being applied directly. Deleting that factor would change the problem. The crossing factor c depends on both choices. It cannot be placed entirely inside either group sum.

$$
\sum_{x_A,x_B} f(x_A)g(x_B)c(x_A,x_B)
$$

What would suffice

We can allow a short list of shared summaries, provided fixing their group contributions leaves independent sums. The summaries must be kept exactly. This is the structure we will ask our new calculations to have; we are not assuming the original formula already has it. Next we will explain why answers to different weighted calculations can determine the original answer.

$$
\text{condition on summaries}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{separate}
$$

06. What makes a node inexpensive?

Purpose: we now need a guarantee that applies to every input, not just the example.

Balanced local work

We are here because we now need a guarantee that applies to every input, not just the example. Let N be the bounded-occurrence input size. At a selected node t, partition its variable indices into r groups, each of size at most 2N/r. Assign every factor to one group. Here r is a fixed integer at least two. Every input of a factor uses that same assigned group.

$$
[N]=\bigsqcup_{g=1}^{r}S_{t,g},\quad |S_{t,g}|\leq2N/r
$$

Bounded exact summaries

Allow at most 2τN integer linear measurements at each node. Each is supported in one group and has coefficient absolute sum at most D, hence integer values in [−D,D]. The density tau is a positive rational, and D a positive integer. Both are fixed before the input. Fix each group contribution separately, not just their total.

$$
\#\text{measurements}\leq2\tau N,\quad\|m\|_1\leq D
$$

Name the cost

Enumerate summary tuples, then enumerate assignments within each group separately. Multiply the group answers. The searches add; we do not search their Cartesian product. This also works with known constants added to factor inputs, and with specified actual bits fixed inside their own groups. Those extensions will pay for derivatives. Next we need a construction that creates this node structure while keeping the polynomial degree low.

$$
T_{\rm node}\leq\operatorname{poly}(N)(2D+1)^{2\tau N}2^{2N/r}
$$

09. Swapping gives locality—but loses identity.

Purpose: we need each replacement to draw from its factor’s assigned group at a selected node.

Match the group labels

We are here because we need each replacement to draw from its factor’s assigned group at a selected node. At node t, the receiving factor wants inputs supplied by its assigned group. Swapping the two labels makes the supplying label match that receiving label. Try the swap control. It concerns slot labels, not new Boolean variables.

$$

$$

Swap every node

Let Πt exchange the two labels at node t. The swaps commute and square to identity. Their product V gives the matching condition at all nodes. A nonzero entry of the full-swap matrix connects only matching groups.

$$
\Pi_t^2=I,\quad V=\prod_{t=1}^{Q}\Pi_t

Identify the conflict

V is generally not identity. We need a compromise that is close to identity in a low-rank sense and nearly group-matching in an entry-support sense. These are two different notions. The technical spectral lemma supplies both at once. Next we will state that lemma explicitly, then derive the useful deformation from its two guarantees.
